# Scoping questions

Ask these before agreeing to check a tool. The answers decide whether the work is a validation, a monitoring plan or an audit, and whether it can be done at all. Example answers are for the deterioration score at Tonmick Health.

## The claim

1. **What does the tool predict or produce, in one sentence?** *Example: the risk that a ward patient will need intensive care, a higher level of care, or will die, within 48 hours.*
2. **In whom, and at what moment?** *Adults on a general ward, 12 hours after admission. Not intensive care, not comfort care.*
3. **What decision does it feed, and who makes it?** *A score of 60 or more alerts the rapid-response team, who decide whether to evaluate the patient.*
4. **What performance is claimed, by whom, on which patients?** *The vendor's card: C-statistic 0.84 on the vendor's own hospitals. No event rate for our kind of hospital, no subgroup results.*
5. **What is not disclosed?** *The formula.*

## The setting

6. **Is the tool live, silent, or not yet installed?** If live, since when at each site?
7. **Was there a silent period at every site?** *No: one hospital went live without one.*
8. **What has changed since go-live?** Sites added, laboratory methods, versions of the tool, staffing of the response.
9. **Who can say how the outcome is recorded, and how often the record is wrong?**

## The stakes

10. **What happens to a patient when the tool is wrong in each direction?**
11. **Which groups of patients would it be worst to fail?** Name them now, before any result.
12. **What would make the committee stop, change or keep the tool?** If no result could change the decision, say so and do less.

## The work

13. **Who will read the report, and what one page do they need?**
14. **What data can be released, to whom, under what agreement?**
15. **When is the decision due?**

## A first reading of the answers

- No silent period and alerts already live: performance after go-live cannot be read as the tool's accuracy, because a working alert prevents the events it predicted. Plan for that (Chapter 11).
- An outcome nobody has audited: budget a chart review (Chapter 9).
- A group named in question 11 with few patients: say at the start that the data may not be able to answer (Chapter 10).
